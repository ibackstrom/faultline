Discuss this case ↗

A few useful boundaries.

What do you actually do?

One engineering job at a time. You bring a failing agent workflow that's hard to replay. We investigate it within an agreed budget, work toward a repeatable case, and check a proposed fix with the same inputs and normal-use controls. If it reproduces, you keep the test, commands and before/after results. If it doesn't, you get a record of what we tested and what remains unresolved. It's a service, not software.

Do we need to replace our eval tools?

No. If Promptfoo, LangSmith, Braintrust or your own tests already solve the problem, there may be nothing for us to add. The useful job is the reproduction and fix check your current process still leaves to an engineer.

We don’t have a fix yet. Can we still start?

Yes. Start with the failure, what you expected and what it is blocking. We can scope reproduction and investigation first. We propose a correction when the evidence supports one; implementation and any later verification are agreed separately. You don’t need a patch or private logs for the first conversation.

What does the person approving the release get?

A before/after record of the agreed checks: what failed, what changed, which normal paths still worked and what remains untested. Your engineer can rerun the evidence; your release owner can see its limits. It supports a decision about that workflow, not a guarantee that the whole agent is safe or reliable.

What isn't included by default?

Production writes, live third-party security testing, model training and access to private customer records. This isn't a security certification or a promise that the rest of the system is safe. New access, provider spend and testing scope need agreement first.
